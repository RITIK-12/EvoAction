3. Recursive task tree (real dispatch)

Max depth actually reached: 1 (limit 3). Children exist only where an investigator raised an evidence-cited new question that passed the dispatcher's novelty, cycle, depth, fan-out and budget gates. Rejected/deduplicated requests are shown too.

Iteration 1 supervisor: The incumbent workflow severely over-segments the galaxea_011 episode (30 predicted vs 12 reference segments) yielding zero F1 at IoU>=0.75, while the robointer_droid_000010 episode predicts the correct number of segments but with poor boundary alignment (mean boundary MAE ~1.23s). Errors are dominated by boundary misalignments and spurious placements, indicating that the model struggles to detect true subtask boundaries and often inserts false cuts.

Iteration 2 supervisor: The incumbent workflow severely over-segments episodes (e.g., 30 predicted vs 12 reference segments in galaxea_011) while failing to recall any short reference events (<2 s). Most predictions are classified as boundary misaligned or spurious/badly placed, and boundary offsets show many early/late errors. This indicates the model is generating uniformly spaced, short segments that do not correspond to actual manipulation subtasks.

  • [depth 1] Are the incumbent's predicted boundaries temporally aligned with meaningful robot-state change points (gripper actuation or end‑effector pauses) as revealed by telemetry hints? done
    Predicted boundaries show some proximity to gripper actuation hints (e.g., all galaxea_011 gripper hints within 0.43 s of a predicted boundary and two of three robointer_droid hints within 0.15 s), but overall alignment is poor: micro‑F1 is 0.083, mean boundary MAE is 0.75 s, and error breakdown lists many ‘boundary misaligned’ cases (18 in galaxea_011, 1 in robointer_droid). Thus the incumbent’s predictions are not consistently temporally aligned with meaningful robot‑state change points.
    implication: Incorporate telemetry hints (gripper/eef_pause) as explicit guidance in the annotation prompt or increase sampling frequency to better capture rapid state changes, thereby reducing boundary misalignment.
    tools run: dev_failure_profile, episode_predictions, telemetry_hints · cites: EVAL-e05d4bb044, EV-e9cbb73abb, EV-6c5bce8463 · model: nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B
  • [depth 1] Does over‑segmentation arise because many predicted segments are shorter than the temporal resolution of the uniform sampling (i.e., less than two sampled frames), causing the model to hallucinate cuts between adjacent tiles? done
    Over‑segmentation in galaxea_011 occurs because every predicted segment (30 segments) is shorter than two sampled frames (1 s each at 1 Hz), leading the model to hallucinate cuts between adjacent tiles.
    implication: Increase the temporal resolution of the uniform sampling (e.g., raise sampling_hz to ≥2 Hz or increase frames_per_sheet) so that predicted segments are not systematically shorter than two frames, or modify the model/prompt to encourage longer, more coherent segment predictions.
    tools run: dev_failure_profile, episode_predictions, sampling_coverage · cites: EVAL-e05d4bb044, EV-332c022143, EV-5584e1f0d8 · model: nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B
  • [depth 1] What proportion of predicted segments are shorter than the temporal resolution of the current 1 Hz sampling (i.e., <2 sampled frames), and are the predicted segments uniformly spaced across the video? done
    90.9% of predicted segments (30 out of 33) are shorter than 2 sampled frames (<2 s at 1 Hz). Predicted segments are uniformly spaced across each video: in galaxea_011 they are 1‑second intervals covering the video, and in robointer_droid_000010 they are 5‑second intervals (with the last segment slightly longer to fill the duration).
    implication: Consider increasing the sampling rate or applying a post‑processing step to merge sub‑resolution predictions, as the current 1 Hz sampling yields many predicted segments that cannot be resolved.
    tools run: dev_failure_profile, episode_predictions, sampling_coverage · cites: EV-ab30cdbb9c, EV-8a5fe29362, EV-0f142eb507, EVAL-1a54ffa356 · model: nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B
  • [depth 1] Do the predicted boundaries align with robot‑state change points (gripper/eef_pause) derived from telemetry, or are they largely unrelated to such hints? done
    Predicted boundaries are closely aligned with robot‑state change points (gripper/eef_pause) from telemetry; for galaxea_011 each of the 10 telemetry hints lies within 0.5 s of a predicted boundary (distances 0.0–0.43 s), and the binned boundary offsets show a large proportion of predictions within 0.5 s of reference boundaries.
    implication: The current 1 Hz sampling already captures robot‑state changes adequately; no adjustment to sampling rate, tile size, or prompt is needed.

+You are refining the temporal boundaries of a robot manipulation video annotation.
+Task instruction: "$instruction". Video length: $duration_sec s.
+Current segments (JSON): $segments_json
+
+Each following image is a close-up strip of consecutive frames (timestamps printed in yellow) around one current boundary or one robot-telemetry hint:
+$windows
+
+Policy: a boundary belongs at the first frame where the earlier subtask's world-state change is complete (object released and at rest, gripper opened, drawer closed, pour finished).
+For each window choose one action:
+- "keep": the current boundary is already within 0.25 s of the correct moment
+- "move": give boundary_sec, the corrected time (must lie inside the window)
+- "delete": there is no real state change here; the two neighbouring segments are one event
+- "insert": (hint windows only) a distinct completed state change happens here and is missing; give boundary_sec and new_subtask for the segment that starts there
+
+Return only JSON: {"windows": [{"window_id": "W1", "action": "keep|move|delete|insert", "boundary_sec": <float or null>, "new_subtask": <string or null>, "reason": "<one short sentence>"}]}

Limitations

  • 3 held-out episodes: a feasibility result, not statistically established generalization.
  • Temporal Segment F1 evaluates boundaries only; it does not assess the semantic correctness of subtask labels.
  • The outer-boundary-snapped score is an evaluator-only variant; WGO-Bench's official scorer was not available to verify.
  • Costs are provider-reported tokens x listed per-token prices from /v1/models; billing was not reconciled.
